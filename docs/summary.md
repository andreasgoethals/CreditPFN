# The Tabular Foundation Model Paradigm — a literature synthesis

A **Tabular Foundation Model (TFM)** is a neural network — in practice, almost always a Transformer — pretrained once on millions of (usually synthetic) tabular datasets so that, at deployment, it solves a *new* prediction task purely by in-context learning: the training rows are fed in as context and predictions for query rows fall out of a single forward pass, with no gradient updates, no hyperparameter tuning, and (approximately) Bayesian posterior-predictive semantics inherited from the prior it was pretrained on. This matters because tabular data is the substrate of most consequential applied ML — credit scoring, healthcare, churn, pricing — yet had remained the last major modality where deep learning lost to gradient-boosted trees; TFMs have now reversed that verdict on small-to-medium data and turned "fit a model" into "condition a model", making the *prior* (and how one adapts it to a domain) the central object of study. For a project like CreditPFN, which continues pretraining TabPFN on real credit-risk data, this literature is simultaneously the origin story, the recipe book, and the list of open hazards.

## How to read this / TL;DR

- **One idea underlies everything**: Müller 2021 showed that training a Transformer to predict held-out labels on datasets *sampled from a prior* makes its forward pass approximate the Bayesian posterior-predictive distribution (PPD). Every TFM below is an instantiation of this "prior-fitted network" (PFN) trick; the field's real currency is therefore **prior design**, not architecture.
- **The prior is synthetic and causal by construction.** TabPFN v1/v2 sample millions of tables from random structural causal models (SCMs); v2's ~130M synthetic tables sufficed to beat tuned GBDTs on ≤10k-row tasks in one forward pass — the "GBDTs always win on tabular" era ended in 2025 for small data.
- **Scaling happened on two axes at once**: context (rows/features) via architectural moves — bi-attention over feature×datapoint cells (v2), then column-collapsing row embeddings borrowed from TabICL (Qu 2025) that carry v3 and Qu 2026 to ~1M rows — and prior quality via mixture recipes (Zhang 2025's Mitra) and sheer token count (v3's 8T-token SCM prior).
- **Real data re-entered through continued pretraining, not from-scratch training.** Garg 2025 (Real-TabPFN) showed that continued pretraining of TabPFNv2 on 71 curated real tables with L2-SP regularization beats the synthetic-only default; TabDPT (Ma 2026) went further and pretrained on real data alone with SSL + retrieval, exhibiting power-law scaling. This is the direct methodological template for CreditPFN.
- **Per-dataset adaptation is genuinely contested.** Rubachev 2025 finds *full* finetuning beats PEFT/LoRA and works by sharpening query–key retrieval, but is unstable under temporal shift; Tanna 2025/2026 (TabTune benchmark) find finetuning rarely helps across six-seven TFMs, often *hurts* accuracy and calibration — with TabPFN the most tolerant of SFT. Zero-shot is a strong, sometimes unbeatable baseline; always report it.
- **Calibration is a first-class selling point of the paradigm** — the PPD interpretation means TFMs are natively probabilistic, and Zhang et al. 2025 show out-of-the-box TabPFNv2 beats specialized SOTA in semi-supervised estimation, heterogeneous treatment effects, and covariate shift — but adaptation (esp. naive SFT) can destroy calibration, which is exactly the risk axis for PD models.
- **The paradigm extends by re-authoring the prior, not the model**: time-driven SCMs give drift detection/extrapolation (Helli 2024), observational+interventional SCM pairs give causal-effect prediction (Robertson 2025, Ma 2026 CausalFM), biased/fair SCM pairs give fairness (FairPFN), calendar features on top of frozen TabPFN give SOTA zero-shot forecasting (Hoo 2024/2026), and a feature-widening prior gives 30k-feature HDLSS handling (Kolberg 2026).
- **In-context "training" is free retraining** — Rundel 2024 exploits one-forward-pass refits to make Data Shapley, LOCO, and exact-retraining interpretability cheap; BETA (Liu & Ye 2025) and Ye 2025's divide-and-conquer show the frozen model can be patched at inference time (ensembled encoders, bagging, chunked contexts) instead of retrained.
- **The ecosystem matured fast**: educational reimplementations (Pfefferle 2025), unified adaptation tooling (TabTune), AutoGluon shipping Mitra, distillation of TabPFN-2.5 into MLPs/trees for cheap deployment, and fully open competitive stacks (Qu 2026) mean the paradigm is no longer a single-lab artifact.
- **Known failure modes to design around**: instability under temporal/distribution shift after finetuning, quadratic-in-context inference cost, ceilings on classes/features fixed at pretraining time, single-table myopia (Klein & Hoffart 2026 argue real enterprise tables need grounding in code/rules/KGs), and benchmark overfitting to academic splits.
- **Net position for 2026**: the strongest results (TabPFN-2.5, TabPFN-3, Mitra, Qu 2026) all combine a *better synthetic prior* + *more scale* + *optionally a real-data continued-pretraining pass* — precisely the three-ingredient recipe CreditPFN instantiates for one vertical (credit risk: PD classification + LGD regression).

## Timeline & lineage

The field is a single trunk with three branches. **Trunk**: PFN theory (2021) → TabPFN v1 (2023) → TabPFN v2 (2025) → TabPFN-2.5 / TabPFN-3 (2026). **Branch A — scaling** attacks context length and prior quality (TabICL, Mitra, Ye 2025, Qu 2026, Kolberg 2026). **Branch B — adaptation** attacks the frozen-model assumption (Real-TabPFN, Rubachev, BETA, TabTune, TabDPT). **Branch C — extension** re-targets the PFN trick at new problem classes (time series, drift, causal, fairness, interpretability, BO). ## Foundations — Prior-Fitted Networks & Bayesian in-context learning

The entire tabular-foundation-model paradigm rests on a single conceptual move made by Müller et al. (Müller 2021): recasting Bayesian posterior-predictive inference as a supervised learning problem over datasets. The object of interest is the posterior predictive distribution (PPD), p(y|x, D) = ∫ p(y|x, t) p(t|D) dt — the quantity that gives calibrated predictions under a prior over data-generating mechanisms t. For essentially all interesting priors this integral is intractable, and the classical remedies — MCMC (Hoffman 2014) and variational inference (Blei 2017) — are slow and require access to (unnormalized) densities. The Prior-Data Fitted Network (PFN) sidesteps both requirements: it demands only the ability to *sample* from the prior. One repeatedly draws a task t ~ p(t), samples a dataset D ∪ {(x, y)} from it, and trains a transformer to predict the held-out y given (x, D) as context. At deployment, a single forward pass on (training rows, test row) emits an approximation to the PPD. Bayesian inference has been *amortized* into the weights: the model is not learning a task, it is learning the mapping from datasets to posteriors.

What makes this more than a heuristic is the training objective. The prior-data negative log-likelihood — plain cross-entropy on the held-out label — is shown (Müller 2021, Insight 1) to equal, up to an additive constant, the expected KL divergence between the model's output and the true PPD. Two consequences follow that shape everything downstream. First, *lower training loss means closer to the true posterior*: since every step sees freshly sampled synthetic data, there is no meaningful notion of overfitting, and "more pretraining is strictly better." This is the theoretical license later invoked, implicitly or explicitly, by every scaled-up successor — and, for a project like CreditPFN, the justification for continued pretraining on additional (real) data, with the crucial caveat that once the data stream is a *finite* real corpus rather than an inexhaustible sampler, the no-overfitting guarantee evaporates; Müller et al.'s own Omniglot fine-tuning experiment is the first demonstration of that regime. Second, *the model can only be as good as its prior is realistic*. The PPD being approximated is the PPD under p(t); if the prior mismatches real data, the posterior inherits the mismatch. This single limitation generates most of the field's subsequent research program: TabPFN's structural-causal-model priors (Hollmann 2023, 2025), prior-mixing recipes (Zhang 2025), and real-data continued pretraining (Garg 2025) are all responses to it.

The design choices in the original paper are worth dwelling on because nearly all of them survive into modern TFMs. The architecture is a transformer *encoder* without positional encodings, making it permutation-equivariant over dataset rows — the correct inductive bias, since a dataset is a set, not a sequence. Each (x, y) pair is embedded by simple linear layers and summed; training examples attend to each other, while test queries attend only to the training set and not to one another, keeping test predictions conditionally independent and cheap to batch. For regression, the paper introduces the Riemann head — a histogram distribution whose bucket borders equalize prior-data mass, with half-normal tails — proven able to approximate any Riemann-integrable density in KL. This bucketed distributional output (itself borrowed from distributional RL, Bellemare 2017) is the direct ancestor of TabPFNv2's regression head and thus of any PFN-based LGD regressor. Even the preprocessing conventions persist: zero-padding to a fixed feature budget with √(max/used) rescaling, and standardization computed from *training rows only* to avoid test leakage.

The intellectual lineage is explicit. PFNs sit in the tradition of learning-to-learn in a forward pass (Hochreiter 2001; Santoro 2016; Gordon 2019) and of simulation-based/amortized inference (Papamakarios & Murray 2016; Cranmer 2020), and are closest architecturally to Conditional and Attentive Neural Processes (Garnelo 2018; Kim 2018) — which Müller et al. outperform on GP posterior emulation. The differentiator from Neural Processes is less the architecture than the *framing*: the loss is interpreted as PPD approximation under an explicit, exchangeable prior, which is what turns "a set-conditioned predictor" into "a Bayesian inference machine" and, later, licenses the position that PFNs are the future of Bayesian prediction generally (Müller et al. 2025) and the statistics-community reading of TabPFN as approximate Bayesian inference (Zhang et al. 2025).

Why does this map so naturally onto tabular data? Because tabular learning is, in effect, a massive meta-learning problem in disguise: millions of small, heterogeneous datasets exist, each too small to train a deep model on, yet collectively sharing structure (smooth-ish functions, sparse causal dependencies, noise). A PFN converts cross-dataset regularity into a prior and pays the learning cost once, offline. The original paper already demonstrates this: with GP and BNN priors (including a genuinely Bayesian prior *over architectures* — sampling depth, width, activation, then weights), a single pretrained PFN evaluated on 21 small OpenML binary tasks achieved the best mean AUC rank against tuned XGBoost and CatBoost, and — critically for credit risk — the best calibration (ECE 0.025 vs. 0.066 for XGBoost), all in seconds rather than hours. Calibration is not an add-on here; it falls out of the objective, since minimizing KL to the true PPD penalizes miscalibrated confidence directly. For PD estimation, where calibrated probabilities are regulatory-critical, this is arguably the paradigm's strongest native selling point.

Honesty about the 2021 paper's limits sharpens the picture of what came after. The tabular results are proof-of-concept scale: ~100-row datasets with 30 labeled examples, at most 60 features, balanced binary classification only; no real-data regression, no categorical handling, no missing values, no distribution shift. Attention is quadratic in dataset size, capping context. And the GP/BNN priors with median-binarized targets are simplistic — plainly not matched to the conditional structures of real tables. Each of these gaps defines a branch of the subsequent literature: Hollmann (2023, 2025) replaces the prior with SCM-sampled tables and scales the recipe into TabPFN; Qu (2025) and Grinsztajn (2026) attack the quadratic-context ceiling; Helli (2024) extends the prior to model distribution shift; and the synthetic-vs-real-prior tension — the deepest open question the 2021 paper leaves behind — becomes the axis along which Real-TabPFN-style continued pretraining (Garg 2025) and, by extension, domain-specialized efforts like CreditPFN position themselves. The bedrock claim, however, is untouched by any of this: a transformer trained on samples from a prior over datasets *is* an approximate Bayesian inference engine, and everything else in the field is an argument about which prior, at what scale, and with how much real data mixed in.


## The TabPFN model line — architecture & its evolution (v1 → v2 → 2.5 → 3)

The TabPFN line is the concrete instantiation of the Prior-Data Fitted Network idea (Müller 2021): train a Transformer offline, once, on millions of synthetic datasets so that a single forward pass over (X_train, y_train, X_test) approximates the Bayesian posterior predictive distribution under the synthetic prior. Everything in the line's evolution — architecture, prior, preprocessing, ensembling — is downstream of that objective, and each generation can be read as an answer to the previous one's most binding constraint.

**v1 (Hollmann 2023)** established the recipe at toy scale: small classification only (≤1,000 train rows, ≤100 numerical features, ≤10 classes). Architecturally it is a 12-layer, ~25.8M-parameter Transformer in which each *row* (feature vector + label) is one token; permutation invariance comes from having no positional encodings, and variable feature counts are handled crudely by zero-padding to 100 dimensions and rescaling. Attention is split into train↔train self-attention and test→train cross-attention, shrinking cost from (n+m)² to n²+n·m — a masking discipline that persists through every successor and *defines* the ICL semantics (predictions for one test point never depend on other test points). The decisive contribution was the prior: a 50/50 mixture of structural causal models (sparse DAGs read off edge-dropped MLPs, with sampled activations and noise) and BNNs, with an explicit Occam's-razor bias toward small graphs. Classification targets are manufactured by binning a continuous SCM node and shuffling labels. Training on ~9.2M synthetic datasets with cross-entropy on held-out points provably drives the network toward the true PPD. Inference-time machinery — z-normalization on train statistics, Yeo-Johnson power transforms, and a 32-member ensemble over feature-column rotations and label permutations — compensated for the architecture's imperfect invariances. The weaknesses were equally clear: O(n²) row attention, degradation on categorical features and missing values (NaN→0), and no regression at all.

**v2 (Hollmann 2025, Nature)** rebuilt the architecture around the table's 2D structure: each *cell* becomes a token, and twelve layers alternate feature-attention (across a row) with sample-attention (down a column, test attending only to train). This makes the model natively permutation-invariant over both rows and columns, handles variable feature counts without padding hacks, and lets it ingest missing values via an explicit indicator channel. Identically distributed columns are disambiguated by *random* per-feature embeddings resampled at inference — a design choice Ye (2025) later showed acts like in-context one-hot feature IDs, letting the model infer inter-attribute relationships on the fly (early layers attend to the label token, deep layers to semantically related features). Regression arrives via a piecewise-constant "Riemann"/bar-distribution head over ~5,000 quantile bins, giving full (even bimodal) predictive distributions — the mechanism CreditPFN relies on for LGD's point masses at 0 and 1. The prior was scaled and enriched (~130M SCM-generated tables; scale-free DAGs via preferential attachment, tree-split and NN edge mechanisms, categorical discretization, Kumaraswamy warping, MCAR missingness), but remained *purely synthetic* — real data enters the line only later, through Real-TabPFN-style continued pretraining (Garg 2025). Inference adds KV caching of train states (~300× CPU speedup for repeated queries), a 4–8-way ensemble over pre/post-processing variants, and a softmax temperature of 0.9 as a baked-in calibration correction. Zhang et al. (2025) supplied the cleanest independent account of *why* this works — the training loss is an unbiased estimator of the KL to the true PPD — and documented near-nominal coverage and label-noise robustness, but also the hard edges: ~10k-row MSE plateau, ~500-feature cap, ≤10 native classes. Ye (2025) confirmed these edges adversarially (behind plain logistic regression on high-dimensional data, behind CatBoost at scale) and showed they can be partially patched *without retraining* via feature-bagging, decimal class encoding, and tree-routed sub-models — useful cheap baselines, but an implicit argument that real fixes require touching the prior or the architecture.

**TabPFN-2.5 (Grinsztajn 2026, Prior Labs)** chose the conservative fix: same alternating dual attention, scaled to 50k rows × 2k features via deeper stacks (18 regression / 24 classification layers), 3-feature grouped embeddings, 64 learned "thinking rows" (register-token-style extra compute), FlashAttention-3, and per-estimator feature subsampling capped at 500. Its ~50 hyperparameters were tuned by a pleasingly self-referential trick — TabPFN v2 as a regression surrogate over candidate configs. Crucially for this synthesis, 2.5 ships in two flavors: a purely synthetic checkpoint (prior "improved substantially," details proprietary) and **Real-TabPFN-2.5**, continued-pretrained on 43 deduplicated real datasets following Garg (2025) — and it is the real-data variant that beats every tuned-and-ensembled baseline on TabArena classification. Post-hoc temperature scaling and decision-threshold tuning appear as first-class calibration tools, though headline results are uncalibrated and no ECE/Brier numbers are reported — a genuine gap.

**TabPFN-3 (Grinsztajn 2026)** is an architectural break, and a notable convergence with the rival TabICL line (Qu 2025), which had already shown that collapsing columns into fixed-width row embeddings *before* ICL cuts complexity from O(m²n + n²m) to O(m²n + n²). v3 adopts exactly this: inducing-point column embeddings → 4 CLS tokens compressing each row to 512-d → a 24-layer row-level ICL transformer with multi-query test→train attention (8× smaller KV cache), enabling 1M rows on a single H100. Classification becomes an attention-based soft-kNN retrieval decoder (class-count-agnostic up to 160); regression keeps the bar-distribution head with CDF-inversion quantiles. The prior — still 100% synthetic, extended with temporal, spatial, many-class, and OOD components over >8T tokens — beats Real-TabPFN-2.5 on TabArena, directly complicating the Real-TabPFN premise: at the frontier, a better synthetic prior beat real-data continued pretraining *on generic benchmarks* (though 2.5 still wins in the many-features/low-n regime). The trade for CreditPFN is stark: v3's architecture shares almost nothing with v2.x, so a continued-pretraining implementation needs architecture-specific loading, preprocessing, and loss handling and its hyperparameters must be revalidated. CreditPFN now supports both v2.6 and v3, but compatibility is not evidence that Garg's v2 recipe transfers unchanged; the v2.5/v3 weights also carry non-commercial licenses with undisclosed priors.

Finally, **nanoTabPFN (Pfefferle 2025)** distills the v2 architecture to <500 lines — bi-attention, mean-padded target encoder, train/test masking, schedule-free AdamW with ~zero weight decay — trainable in a minute, borrowing TabICL's prior. Its "beats TabPFN" claim is apples-to-oranges (the prior is matched to the tiny eval regime), and it drops regression, categorical handling, and ensembling; but as a mechanistic reference for exactly which components continued pretraining perturbs, it is the best pedagogical artifact the line has produced.


## Scaling the paradigm — rows, features, and the prior itself

TabPFN v2 (Hollmann 2025) proved the concept but drew its own boundaries: roughly 10k rows, 500 features, and quadratic-in-rows attention that makes even those limits expensive. The scaling literature attacks these boundaries along three largely orthogonal axes — architectural compression of rows, substitution or augmentation of the synthetic prior, and brute extension of the pretraining regime — and the interesting story is how these axes interact, and occasionally contradict one another.

The architectural axis is best represented by the TabICL lineage. TabICL (Qu 2025) observed that TabPFN v2's two-dimensional (row × column) attention pays a quadratic price twice, and instead collapses each row's features into a fixed embedding via a column-wise set transformer before running in-context attention over rows — trading exact feature-level interaction for O(n² + nm²) cost and 500k-row capacity. TabICLv2 (Qu 2026) keeps this skeleton but fixes its known pathologies with unusually well-motivated components: repeated feature grouping (columns entered into multiple circularly-shifted groups) restores the feature asymmetry the compression destroys; target-aware embedding injects train-row labels *before* the column transformer, making even the embedding stage an in-context learner; and QASSMax, a query-gated generalization of Scalable Softmax, counters attention fading at long context so the model generalizes to 60k-sample contexts it barely saw in pretraining. The result is striking: an open model, pretrained for ~24.5 H100 GPU-days, that beats the tuned-and-ensembled RealTabPFN-2.5 untuned, runs ~10× faster, and handles 1M rows × 500 features via disk offloading. Crucially, TabICLv2's own ablations show the *prior* is the largest single effect — a new SCM family with non-tree Cauchy DAGs, eight function types, and explicit data filtering (dropping datasets an ExtraTrees model cannot beat) — and that the architecture diverges when trained on the old TabICL prior. Architecture and prior are not separable design decisions; they co-adapt.

That finding motivates the second axis: treating the prior itself as the object of study. Mitra (Zhang 2025) is the first attempt to replace folklore ("mix in some tree priors for diversity", as TabForestPFN and TabICL did with hand-set 70/30 weights) with a quantitative selection framework: pretrain one model per candidate prior, measure a Generalizability Matrix (cross-prior transfer) and a Performance Vector (real-data AUC), and admit priors that are performant, hard to overfit, and not already covered by the mixture. The resulting 50/50 SCM/tree mixture — including a novel directly-sampled random forest — edges out Attic and TabPFNv2 on ≤10k-row benchmarks, and the framework correctly predicts its own weakest component (DSRF ranks second standalone but adds least marginally). But Mitra also exposes the cost of prior-centric thinking done cheaply: it pretrains on at most 16 features and 640 rows, and consequently *loses* to TabPFNv2 on wide regression tasks; the authors concede that much of its large finetuning gain merely compensates for this pretrain/downstream gap. The greedy selection is also run against a single benchmark family (TabRepo), so the "principled" mixture carries meta-overfitting risk — the framework is sounder than its instantiation.

TabDPT (Ma 2026) takes the opposite bet on the same question: abandon the synthetic prior entirely and pretrain on 123 real OpenML tables via self-supervision. Because each real table has only one target, TabDPT manufactures tasks by masking random columns as pseudo-targets (binned into classification 30% of the time) and subsampling/shuffling features — and, distinctively, aligns training with inference by constructing contexts as FAISS-retrieved local neighbourhoods of a query row, so the model learns to reason over exactly the kind of context it will see at test time. This yields the first clean Chinchilla-style scaling law for TFMs (α≈0.42, β≈0.39) and small but real wins over TabPFN v2 on CC18/CTR23 at equal compute, with an honest appendix documenting failed ideas (cell tokenization, NaN tokens, feature-name embeddings). Yet retrieval is also how TabDPT *avoids* rather than solves the scaling problem: per-query neighbourhoods keep contexts small regardless of table size, at the cost of deployment complexity, an i.i.d.-rows assumption, and hard caps (100 features via lossy PCA, 10 classes via digit decomposition) that TabPFN v2 exceeds natively. The real-data ceiling — 123 datasets versus TabICLv2's ~35M synthetic ones — is the structural weakness of the approach, and its zero calibration reporting (shared, notably, by TabICLv2) leaves the reliability of real-data-trained TFMs unquantified.

The row/feature frontier is completed by two extension efforts. On rows, Prior Labs' own trajectory — TabPFN-2.5 to 50k rows/2k features (Grinsztajn/Prior Labs 2026) and TabPFN-3's TabICL-style row compression to 1M rows on a purely synthetic 8T-token prior (Grinsztajn 2026) — effectively concedes the architectural point to Qu while doubling down on synthetic data. On features, TabPFN-Wide-style work (Kolberg 2026) inverts the usual continued-pretraining recipe: instead of real data, it continues pretraining TabPFNv2 on a synthetic *feature-widening* prior to reach 30k+ features in high-dimensional low-sample regimes (omics-like data), showing that continued pretraining is a general mechanism for shifting a TFM's operating envelope, not just for injecting real-world signal.

Stepping back, the field has an unresolved argument about where scale should come from. TabDPT and Real-TabPFN (Garg 2025) argue real data carries structure synthetic priors miss; TabICLv2 counters by beating RealTabPFN-2.5 with zero real data, attributing the gains to prior design, Muon optimization, and attention scaling — implying real-data continued pretraining may partly compensate for a fixable prior deficit rather than add irreducible signal. Mitra sits between them: better synthetic mixtures help, but its finetuning-dependence shows the pretrain/downstream gap is real and must be closed *somewhere* — by richer priors, real corpora, or downstream adaptation. For a project like CreditPFN this tension is not academic: it predicts that domain continued pretraining should help most precisely where the target domain is *distinctive* relative to the prior (a hypothesis Mitra's generalizability-matrix methodology could test directly on credit data), while TabICLv2 warns that a sufficiently good synthetic backbone may erase much of that headroom. Meanwhile the collective silence on calibration — none of TabICLv2, TabDPT, or Mitra reports ECE or Brier scores despite log-loss training — and TabICLv2's mean-imputation of missing values (hostile to the informative missingness typical of credit files) mark exactly the gaps a domain-specialized, calibration-audited model can occupy.


## Adapting a pretrained TFM — finetuning, continued pretraining, LoRA, tooling

Once a synthetic-prior TFM like TabPFNv2 exists, the field splits along a distinction that the papers themselves rarely draw explicitly but that matters enormously in practice: *per-dataset finetuning* (adapt the weights to one downstream task) versus *corpus-level continued pretraining* (adapt the prior itself on a collection of real tables, then keep using pure in-context learning downstream). Real-TabPFN (Garg 2025) defines the second path; Rubachev (2025) and the two Lexsi Labs studies (Tanna 2025; Tanna 2026) map the first; TabPFN-Wide (Kolberg 2026) shows the continued-pretraining recipe transfers even back to synthetic data; and BETA (Liu & Ye 2025) offers a third, weight-frozen alternative.

**Continued pretraining.** Garg (2025) starts from the released synthetic-only TabPFNv2 checkpoint and runs a second training stage exclusively on 71 manually curated *large* real tables (10k–100k rows) from OpenML and Kaggle — explicitly a "don't stop pretraining" (Gururangan 2020) move, and deliberately two-stage rather than mixing synthetic and real data during pretraining (the contrast case being D'souza 2025). The recipe is unusually concrete: AdamW at a very low LR of 3e-7 with linear warm-up and cosine annealing, 20,000 steps at batch size 1 (one dataset per step, avoiding cross-dataset padding), a 60/40 context/query split with up to 20,000 context rows capped at 400k cells, and — crucially — an L2-SP penalty (λ=0.003) pulling weights back toward the base checkpoint to prevent catastrophic forgetting. The central ablation finding is that *dataset size during CPT is the active ingredient*: gains rise monotonically as context grows from 2,048 to 20,000 rows, curated large OpenML+Kaggle tables give +0.022 normalized ROC-AUC, while a CommonCrawl corpus of tiny tables actually *hurts* (−0.003). Calibration (ECE, cross-entropy) improves alongside ROC — a rare and, for credit scoring, valuable result. The weaknesses are equally concrete: classification only (no regression), 29 small eval datasets, no sensitivity analysis on λ/LR/steps, and modest absolute gains (0.929→0.932 raw ROC).

Kolberg (2026) validates that this recipe is a *template*, not a one-off: TabPFN-Wide applies the identical continued-pretraining structure (full-parameter, warmup+cosine, ~10k steps) but swaps Garg's real corpus for a synthetic feature-widening prior built on TabICL's open SCM generator, extending TabPFNv2 to 30k+ features without forgetting (Spearman ρ=0.9935 against the base model on original-range tasks — a forgetting check worth copying). Notably, its optimizer settings *diverge* from Garg's: LR 1e-5 and weight decay 1e-4 versus 3e-7 and L2-SP. Neither paper tunes these, so the "correct" CPT hyperparameters remain genuinely unsettled; the plausible reconciliation is that Kolberg's synthetic data is unbounded and on-distribution (tolerating a hotter LR), while Garg's 71 real tables demand conservatism against overfitting and forgetting. Kolberg also flags the regressor as untouched future work — the same gap Garg leaves, and precisely where a credit-LGD contribution sits.

**Per-dataset finetuning.** Rubachev (2025) settles a question left ambiguous since TabPFNv1: does one need parameter-efficient/partial finetuning to avoid destroying the prior? Against the TabPFNv1-era folklore (TuneTables, Feuer 2024), the answer for v2 is no: full finetuning matches every PEFT variant (LoRA, last-layers, LN+embeddings+head) in accuracy while converging fastest, and is therefore the recommended default. The protocol details are directly reusable — LR tuned over logspace(5e-6, 5e-4), full train set as context with 1024 objects per step carrying the loss (larger prediction batches demonstrably help), patience-16 early stopping, and a ×5 finetuned deep ensemble delivering the single largest gain (mean rank 1.05 on academic benchmarks). The paper's mechanistic contribution is that finetuning works by *sharpening the model's implicit retrieval*: query–key attention aligns better with target similarity and attention entropy drops. That sharpening cuts both ways — on TabReD's temporally shifted datasets (including Homecredit Default) TabPFNv2 is less stable than TabM and finetuning sometimes degrades performance, and the paper reports *no* calibration metrics at all, leaving open whether lower-entropy, more confident attention harms probability quality.

The Tanna papers fill exactly that calibration hole, at the cost of shallower tuning. TabTune (Tanna 2025) is the tooling contribution — a scikit-learn-style library unifying zero-shot ICL, episodic meta-learning, full SFT, and LoRA (r=8, α=16, dropout 0.05) across seven TFMs — and its companion benchmark (Tanna 2026) is the empirical payload: adaptation outcomes are strongly *architecture-dependent*. Full SFT is near-catastrophic for TabICL and Orion-BiX (TabZilla accuracy 0.873→0.567) yet TabPFN survives it essentially intact, and is uniquely the model whose calibration stays flat under full finetuning (ECE 0.0276 zero-shot → 0.0287 after SFT, while SFT triples–quadruples ECE elsewhere). Their TabPFN SFT defaults (AdamW, lr 1e-5, wd 1e-4, ≤10–25 epochs) sit between Garg and Rubachev; LoRA they find *unstable* for TabPFN specifically (batched-inference constraints force auto-fallback to full FT), which converges with Rubachev's conclusion from the opposite direction — LoRA buys nothing on TabPFNv2 and may cost something. The regime breakdown is practically useful: finetuning pays off on medium (1k–10k rows) and wide (>100 features) datasets, while on <1k rows zero-shot wins outright. But the honesty caveats are real: four pages, one hyperparameter setting per strategy (TabICL's "collapse" may be under-tuning, not physics), Mitra's broken numbers suggest harness bugs, PEFT calibration tables duplicate non-PEFT rows, and the authors' own Orion models top their own leaderboards.

**The frozen-weights alternative.** BETA (Liu & Ye 2025) shows adaptation need not touch the prior at all: a lightweight learnable input encoder (2-layer MLP with periodic activations, Batch-Ensemble multi-path diversity) plus inference-time bagging lifts TabPFN's feature/class caps and cuts both bias and variance at 0.6 MB of trainable parameters and sub-second inference. It is a compelling PEFT-style baseline, but it targets v1, classification only, and fixed 1k contexts — it complements rather than competes with the CPT line.

**Synthesis for a credit-domain specialization.** The papers agree that (i) TabPFN is the architecture that tolerates gradient adaptation best, in accuracy *and* calibration (Rubachev 2025; Tanna 2025, 2026); (ii) full-parameter updates beat LoRA for it; (iii) gains grow with rows, and large-context/large-dataset training is the lever (Garg 2025; Rubachev 2025). They disagree — or rather, never confronted each other — on optimizer aggressiveness (3e-7+L2-SP vs 1e-5+wd 1e-4) and on whether finetuning-induced retrieval sharpening threatens calibration under the temporal drift endemic to credit portfolios (Rubachev's Homecredit instability vs Tanna's stable ECE, measured only on i.i.d. splits). And all of them, without exception, leave regression untested — the LGD half of any credit specialization proceeds without a published recipe to lean on.

## Extending TFMs to new problems — time series, causal inference, fairness, interpretability, drift

Once TabPFN established that a Transformer prefit on a synthetic prior approximates Bayesian posterior-predictive inference in one forward pass (Müller 2021; Hollmann 2023), the field discovered that this recipe factorizes cleanly into two reusable parts: the *architecture-plus-ICL mechanism* (permutation-invariant attention over context rows, a Riemann/bar-distribution head for regression) and the *prior* that defines what "Bayesian" means. Every extension in this section exploits one of three strategies: (i) **freeze everything and reframe the task as tabular prediction**, (ii) **keep the architecture and training objective but swap the synthetic prior**, or (iii) **exploit the ICL mechanism itself as an algorithmic primitive**. Which strategy a paper picks — and what it must sacrifice — is the organizing question.

**Pure reuse: time series as feature engineering.** The Hoo line is the cleanest demonstration of strategy (i). Hoo (2024) takes the frozen TabPFN-v2 regressor (11M parameters, zero time-series pretraining) and turns univariate forecasting into i.i.d. tabular regression: each timestep becomes a row, features are derived purely from timestamps (a running index plus sine/cosine-encoded calendar components), and the full horizon is predicted non-autoregressively in one pass. This beats Chronos-Large — 65× larger and pretrained on real time-series corpora — on aggregate relative MASE, and the decomposition is telling: Chronos wins on datasets overlapping its pretraining corpus, while TabPFN-TS wins decisively on the 17 true zero-shot datasets. Hoo (2026) hardens this into a real method — adding automatic FFT-derived seasonal features, quantile forecasts from the Riemann head, and, crucially, known-future covariates as first-class columns, where it becomes best-in-class on fev-bench's covariate tasks. Two findings from the 2026 paper reverberate across the whole synthesis. First, swapping the backbone for TabDPT (Ma 2026), pretrained purely on *real* OpenML tables, substantially degrades transfer — the authors attribute TabPFN-v2's edge to the diversity of its synthetic prior. This is the sharpest existing counterpoint (not contradiction — Chronos still wins in-domain) to the Real-TabPFN/TabDPT real-data argument, and it directly informs how gently a continued-pretraining recipe like CreditPFN's should move off the synthetic prior. Second, the method structurally *cannot extrapolate trends*: predictions stay inside the convex hull of context targets, because pretraining draws context and query from the same distribution. Nothing is re-trained here; the costs are latency (O(L²) attention per series) and that convex-hull ceiling.

**Prior swaps: drift, causality, fairness, BO.** Strategy (ii) — retrain from scratch on a purpose-built synthetic prior — began with PFNs4BO (Müller 2023), which replaced the tabular prior with GP/BNN/HEBO priors to make a PFN a drop-in GP surrogate for Bayesian optimization, pioneering tricks (Riemann regression head, style-token user priors, spurious-dimension injection) that later extensions inherit. Helli (2024) applies the same move to temporal drift: TabPFN's SCM prior is augmented with a second-order SCM that, driven by a domain index, sparsely shifts edge weights of the base SCM over time, so covariate, label and concept shift all arise naturally and the model learns to *extrapolate* decision boundaries into unseen future domains. Inference is untouched; only the prior changed. The honest reading of the results is that calibration gains (ECE 0.119→0.091) and synthetic-benchmark gains are large, but real-world gains are marginal and depend on ground-truth domain indices — a caveat that matters for credit vintages, which are natural but imperfect domain labels.

The causal extensions push the prior-swap logic furthest, because the quantity amortized is no longer the observational predictive. Do-PFN (Robertson 2025) samples SCMs, generates *paired* observational and interventional data (same noise, mutilated graph), and trains the PFN to predict interventional outcomes from observational context — so ICL delivers p(y|do(t),x) with no causal graph, no unconfoundedness, and calibrated unidentifiability uncertainty, from a 7.3M-parameter model. FairPFN (Robertson 2025), its sibling, uses the identical paired-target trick for counterfactual fairness: each synthetic SCM is forward-passed twice, once normally (biased data) and once with the protected attribute's outgoing edges zeroed (fair target), teaching the transformer the mapping X_biased → y_fair. Both show a PFN prior can encode a *task transformation*, not merely a data distribution — but both are binary-only, validated mostly on synthetic or circularly-evaluated real data. CausalFM (Ma 2026) then directly attacks Do-PFN's foundation: Theorem 4.3 proves that a prior mixing identifiable and non-identifiable SCMs yields asymptotically non-informative posteriors, so one must train one PFN per identifiable setting (back-door, front-door, IV) with the prior constrained to identifiability-respecting graphs. It beats Do-PFN on front-door tasks, yet its own misspecification ablations show a surprisingly small penalty and it loses to CausalPFN on ACIC2016 — the theory's practical bite remains contested, and the "one model per setting" design arguably abandons the foundation-model promise.

**ICL as an algorithmic primitive.** Rundel (2024) needs neither reframing nor retraining: because "retraining" a PFN on any row/column subset is just a forward pass with a different context, classically infeasible interpretability methods become exact and cheap. LOCO becomes one pass per feature; Kernel SHAP can use *exact* subset-retraining instead of independence-assuming imputation (lower error and variance per compute budget); Data Shapley over rows selects high-value context beyond the 1024-row ceiling. This is the same insight — the context is a mutable program input — that Ye (2025) exploits in the other direction, dissecting TabPFN-v2's randomized attribute tokens and lifting its feature/class/scale limits via test-time divide-and-conquer, effectively "unleashing" the frozen model rather than modifying it. For regulated credit scoring, Rundel's exact-retraining SHAP is the most directly deployable artifact, though demonstrated only at toy scale (≤256 rows, 6 features, binary classification).

**What the map says.** The common thread is that almost nothing here touches the weights of an existing model: extensions either freeze TabPFN entirely or retrain a small PFN from scratch on a bespoke prior. Continued pretraining on real data — the Real-TabPFN/CreditPFN route — is conspicuously *orthogonal* to this entire section: a drift-aware prior (Helli) could in principle be composed with real-data continued pretraining, but nobody has done it, and the Hoo/TabDPT evidence warns that domain specialization buys in-domain gains at the price of narrowed inductive bias. The recurring weaknesses are also shared: O(n²) context limits (unsolved in every paper here), binary-classification-only scope in the fairness/causal line, prior-realism dependence wherever real-world validation is thin, and — with Helli and Do-PFN as partial exceptions — a habit of claiming Bayesian uncertainty without ever evaluating calibration.


## Position, critique & open problems

Three position papers bracket the field's self-understanding, and reading them in sequence reveals both an expanding ambition and a set of unresolved tensions that the empirical corpus has not closed.

The founding manifesto is van Breugel & van der Schaar (2024), which named the agenda: tabular data dominates science and industry yet was nearly absent from foundation-model research, and — unlike LLMs — SOTA remained within academic compute reach. Its lasting contribution is not a model but a specification: four desiderata (mixed-type columns; cross-dataset modelling over heterogeneous schemas; use of textual metadata; column-order invariance) plus the argument that LLMs are a poor substrate for tables — naive tokenization destroys numeric continuity, autoregressive decoding is slow and context-bound, and LLM sampling is poorly calibrated. The paper explicitly rebuts Grinsztajn (2022)'s pessimism that tabular data lacks exploitable cross-dataset structure, holding up TabPFN (Hollmann 2023) as the existence proof. Crucially for domain specialization, the authors flag that web corpora like TabLib omit exactly the proprietary, sensitive tables (medical, financial) where impact would be largest — the gap that Real-TabPFN (Garg 2025) and, by extension, CreditPFN attack via continued pretraining on curated real data. But the paper's own bet — that the key open target is a *conditional generative* LTM — has largely not been how the field went: the winning line (TabPFNv2, TabICL, TabPFN-2.5/3, Mitra) is discriminative ICL over synthetic priors, and the generative agenda remains mostly unbuilt.

Müller et al. (2025) then supplies the theoretical self-justification for that winning line: PFNs convert exponentially growing compute into approximate Bayesian prediction, with the prior specified *declaratively* by a sampling program rather than a tractable density. Its endorsement of post-hoc fine-tuning "in domains where a lot of related datasets are available" is the closest thing the corpus offers to a first-principles rationale for the Real-TabPFN/CreditPFN recipe. What makes the paper valuable, though, is its unusual candor about failure modes, several of which the rest of the corpus confirms rather than fixes. First, *ill-defined support*: nobody can say ex ante which datasets a PFN's prior covers, and heavy-tailed, mixed-scale features (ubiquitous in credit data) sit uneasily in it — Ye (2025)'s dissection and the preprocessing machinery in TabPFNv2 are workarounds, not solutions. Second, the model *cannot count duplicated evidence* (no positional information), a small but genuine departure from Bayesian updating that connects to Falck (2024)'s Martingale critique: the "PFNs are Bayesian" framing is an approximation whose error is unquantified. Third, the paper concedes that as n grows the prior's influence shrinks and tree-based inductive biases may win — which reframes the entire scaling race (Qu 2025; Grinsztajn/Prior Labs 2026; Grinsztajn 2026; Qu 2026) as necessary engineering whose *statistical* payoff is not guaranteed.

Klein & Hoffart (2026) positions itself explicitly against van Breugel & van der Schaar's isolated-table scope: real enterprise tables are generated by code, policies, and workflows, and a statistical prior can only fuzzily approximate a hard `if amount >= 5000` rule. Their proposed FMSLT class generalizes the PFN philosophy — synthetic pretraining on rule→row pairs, then zero-shot inference with retrieved operational artifacts — and cites Real-TabPFN approvingly while arguing real-data pretraining alone is insufficient. For credit risk the critique bites: PD/LGD data is shaped by underwriting cutoffs and Basel/IFRS9 policy, so a purely correlational prior blurs decision boundaries that are in fact deterministic. But the paper must be heavily discounted: zero experiments, an "Operational Turing Test" defined but never built, an acknowledged unsolved provenance gap, and an SAP-shaped view of what tables look like. It is a research program, not evidence.

Where Klein & Hoffart supply a program, BeyondArena (Purucker et al. 2026) supplies the evidence: the most systematic demonstration yet that TFM generality is regime-bound. Across 142 curated datasets, the in-context learning of TabPFN-2.6, TabICLv2, and TabDPT is top-ranked on IID, tiny/small, and low-dimensional data, but on both non-IID sub-benchmarks — temporal and grouped splits — tuned-and-ensembled RealMLP wins, with tuned CatBoost taking high-cardinality data; the best-GBDT-over-best-TFM margin grows with sample size (Spearman ρ=+0.60) and high-cardinality categorical columns (ρ=+0.47), and TFMs reach peak performance on only a small fraction of temporal, grouped, and large datasets. This scales up Rubachev et al.'s TabReD warning that random splits flatter deep and foundation models under temporal shift, and sits alongside Klein & Hoffart's (2026) grounding charge and Tanna's (2026) finding that naive fine-tuning degrades calibration. Notably, BeyondArena tests in-context learning only, explicitly leaving fine-tuning open — so whether adaptation closes the non-IID gap, or per Rubachev's (2025) fine-tuning results amplifies instability under shift, remains the field's central open question; that TabPFN's own developers (Prior Labs) co-authored this negative result lends it weight.

Stepping back, several honest weaknesses recur across the whole corpus and deserve to be stated plainly. (1) **Evaluation fragility.** Headline results live on academic benchmarks (AMLB, TabArena) with i.i.d. splits; Purucker (2026) shows systematically that TFM ICL loses to tuned baselines the moment splits become temporal or grouped, Rubachev (2025) shows fine-tuned TabPFNv2 is unstable under temporal shift, and Tanna (2026) finds fine-tuning *often hurts* accuracy and calibration across six TFMs — directly qualifying the Real-TabPFN success story and warning that adaptation gains are model- and regime-specific. (2) **Contamination risk.** When pretraining touches real corpora (Garg 2025; Ma 2026's TabDPT), few-shot numbers can be inflated by leakage — a concern van Breugel & van der Schaar raised in 2024 that no paper has audited rigorously. (3) **Calibration is asserted more than measured.** "Well-calibrated uncertainty" is a standard PFN claim (Müller et al. 2025), yet systematic ECE/Brier reporting is rare, and Tanna (2026) shows adaptation can degrade calibration silently — a first-order issue for PD estimation. (4) **The synthetic-vs-real question is unresolved.** Garg (2025) says real data helps; Grinsztajn (2026) reaches SOTA at 1M rows on a *purely synthetic* 8T-token prior; Zhang (2025) suggests the answer is prior *mixing* discipline rather than realness per se. The field lacks a controlled study disentangling prior diversity, realness, and scale. (5) **Position papers outrun evidence.** All three papers here contain no experiments of consequence; their influence is agenda-setting, and the community has partially validated (PFN scaling, real-data continued pretraining) and partially ignored (generative LTMs, systemic grounding) their programs.

The open problems, then, are concrete: characterizing prior support and when specialization helps versus overfits the prior; adaptation methods that survive temporal shift and preserve calibration; leakage-audited evaluation on genuinely held-out, non-academic data; and — furthest out — grounding statistical priors in the rule systems that generate operational data. CreditPFN sits squarely on the first three: it is a domain-specialization experiment whose value depends precisely on measuring what the position papers assume.


## Cross-cutting design axes

The field can be decomposed into five nearly-orthogonal design choices. Every paper in the corpus is a point in this space, and most disagreements between papers are disagreements about *one* axis while holding the others fixed.

| Axis | Option A | Option B | Option C | Representative evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **(a) Prior** | Pure synthetic SCM/BNN (Müller 2021; Hollmann 2023/2025; Qu 2025/2026; Grinsztajn 2026-v3) | Pure real data + SSL (Ma 2026 TabDPT) | Mixed: synthetic base + real-data continued pretraining (Garg 2025; Grinsztajn 2026-v2.5) | TabPFN-3 (synthetic) beats Real-TabPFN-2.5 on TabArena, but TabDPT shows real+SSL beats synthetic at equal compute — unresolved |
| **(b) Architecture** | Row-as-token 1D encoder (Müller 2021; Hollmann 2023 v1; TabDPT) | Cell-as-token 2D row×feature attention (Hollmann 2025 v2; Zhang 2025 Mitra; Pfefferle 2025) | Column-embed-then-row-ICL, 3-stage (Qu 2025/2026 TabICL; Grinsztajn 2026-v3) | v3 abandoning v2's 2D attention for TabICL's compression is the field's clearest architectural convergence |
| **(c) Context/scaling** | Hard caps, full context (v1: 1k×100; v2: 10k×500×10cls; v2.5: 50k×2k) | Compression/curriculum to 500k–1M rows (TabICL, TabICLv2, v3) | Retrieval/selection: kNN contexts (TabDPT, LocalPFN), data valuation (Rundel 2024), divide-and-conquer (Ye 2025) | O(n²) attention is the original sin; every scaling paper is a workaround for it |
| **(d) Inference** | Single forward pass + permutation/preprocessing ensembling (v1: 32-way; v2: 4/8-way; TabICL: 32) | Retrieval per query (TabDPT FAISS) | Post-hoc: temperature scaling, threshold tuning (v2.5/v3), distillation to MLP/trees (v2.5/v3) | Ye 2025: randomized-token equivariance means permutation ensembling yields only ~0.3% — don't over-invest |
| **(e) Adaptation** | Frozen ICL (default everywhere) | Per-dataset gradient FT: full FT ≻ LoRA/partial (Rubachev 2025; Tanna 2025/2026) | Corpus-level continued pretraining (Garg 2025; Kolberg 2026) or input-adapter PEFT (Liu & Ye 2025 BETA) | Full FT ≈ PEFT in accuracy but faster to converge (Rubachev 2025); LoRA on TabPFN is unstable (Tanna 2025) |

**On priors.** The theoretical contract (Müller 2021: the PFN loss *is* KL to the true posterior predictive) means model quality is bounded by prior realism — and the field has split into three responses. The purist line keeps improving synthetic priors: tree-SCM mixtures (Qu 2025: 70/30 MLP/XGBoost; Zhang 2025 Mitra formalizes this with a generalizability-matrix framework replacing folklore), richer activations, temporal/OOD priors (Grinsztajn 2026-v3). The realist line argues real tables carry structure synthetic samplers miss: TabDPT (Ma 2026) trains from scratch on 123 real datasets with column-masking SSL and matches v2; Real-TabPFN (Garg 2025) is the compromise — synthetic base, then gentle CPT (lr 3e-7, L2-SP λ=0.003) on 71 large real tables, with gains that scale monotonically with CPT context size (2k→20k rows). Critically, the direction of transfer matters: Hoo 2024/2026 show the *synthetic* prior wins zero-shot out-of-domain (TabPFN-TS beats real-data-pretrained Chronos on true zero-shot tasks, and beats a TabDPT-TS backbone swap outright), while real-data pretraining pays off *in-domain* (Chronos wins on its own pretraining datasets). That asymmetry is the strongest cross-paper argument that CPT's value proposition is domain specialization, not generic uplift — and Qu 2026 (TabICLv2 beating Real-TabPFN-2.5 with pure synthetic data + Muon) plus TabPFN-3 sharpen this: a better prior/optimizer can erase generic real-data gains at the frontier.

**On architecture.** Three generations: rows-as-tokens (cheap, no per-cell structure; v1, TabDPT), cells-as-tokens with alternating row/feature attention (expressive, O(m²n + n²m) — the cost that caps v2 at 10k rows), and the compress-columns-first design (TabICL's set-transformer column embeddings + CLS-token row vectors → O(m²n + n²) ICL). TabPFN-3 adopting TabICL's skeleton is a genuine convergence event, but it buys row-scaling at the price of a feature-compression bottleneck (v3 underperforms Real-TabPFN-2.5 in wide-feature/low-n regimes). Positional-encoding choices leak here too: TabICL's RoPE breaks column-permutation invariance (patched only by ensembling), whereas v2's random feature-ID tokens preserve it (Ye 2025's mechanistic analysis).

**On inference and calibration.** The paradigm's native asset is the Bayesian posterior predictive: low ECE from the start (Müller 2021: ECE 0.025 vs XGB 0.066), distributional regression via Riemann/bar heads (v2's ~5000-bin head; TabICLv2's 999-quantile pinball alternative), near-nominal coverage (Zhang 2025 "One Model"). But calibration is *bolted on* at the frontier: v2 uses an ad-hoc T=0.9; v2.5/v3 add post-hoc temperature scaling and threshold tuning yet report headline results uncalibrated and publish no ECE. Inference cost is the recurring hidden tax: ensembling multiplies passes, Ye 2025's extensions cost 32×–C×, and TabPFN-TS averaged 2210s per series vs Chronos's 8s.

**On adaptation.** A clean hierarchy has emerged. (1) Frozen ICL is already SOTA on small data and best-calibrated (Tanna 2025). (2) Per-dataset full FT beats LoRA/partial FT — Rubachev 2025 overturns the v1-era "partial FT to avoid overfitting" folklore, and shows mechanistically that FT sharpens the model's implicit retrieval (attention entropy drops); but this works *only* for TabPFN-family architectures — SFT collapses TabICL (0.847→0.567 on TabZilla; Tanna 2026), a strong architecture×adaptation interaction. (3) Corpus-level CPT (Garg 2025; Kolberg 2026's synthetic-widening variant proves the recipe transfers, with Spearman ρ=0.9935 no-forgetting checks) is the only regime that improves the *prior itself* rather than one downstream fit. (4) Input-adapter PEFT (BETA, Liu & Ye 2025) is the cheap outsider: 0.6MB trainable, lifts feature/class caps, but v1-only and classification-only.

## Recurring tensions & weaknesses of the paradigm

**1. The scaling wall is real and only partially breached.** The n≳10k performance plateau (Zhang 2025, citing Nagler) is not just memory: as rows grow, the prior's influence shrinks and trees' inductive bias reasserts itself (Müller 2025 concedes this). Even the extended models lose at scale — frozen v2 ranks behind CatBoost (3.97 vs 1.89) on large data and behind *logistic regression* on high-dim data (Ye 2025), and v3's row-compression trades away the simultaneous many-rows-AND-many-features case. Every scaling fix (retrieval, compression, chunking, divide-and-conquer) sacrifices something: exactness, permutation invariance, or feature width.

**2. The synthetic-prior realism gap cuts both ways and nobody has settled it.** v1 demonstrably under-served categoricals, missingness, and uninformative features (Hollmann 2023's own worst cases); TabDPT shows real data carries missed signal; Real-TabPFN shows CPT helps. Yet TabICLv2 and TabPFN-3 beat the real-data checkpoints with better synthetic priors, and Hoo 2026's ablation shows real-data pretraining *narrows* inductive bias (TabDPT-TS ≪ TabPFN-TS). The honest reading: real data helps when eval and CPT corpus are distributionally close, and prior engineering helps everywhere else — but no paper runs the controlled comparison (same compute, same base, synthetic-improvement vs real-CPT), because the frontier priors are proprietary (v2.5/v3's prior undisclosed; v2's generation code unreleased). Mitra's admission that finetuning gains "partly just compensate for the pretrain/downstream gap" (Zhang 2025) suggests some reported adaptation wins are artifacts of deliberately small pretraining regimes.

**3. Calibration is claimed everywhere and measured almost nowhere.** This is the paradigm's most glaring evaluation failure given that calibrated PPDs are its founding selling point. Rubachev 2025 (the definitive FT study) reports zero calibration metrics while showing FT makes attention *more confident* — sharpening that plausibly harms ECE, untested. TabDPT, TabICLv2, Kolberg 2026, Ye 2025, and both Grinsztajn 2026 reports omit ECE/Brier entirely. Only Tanna 2025/2026 measure calibration under adaptation — finding TabPFN uniquely stable (ECE 0.027–0.047 across regimes) while SFT triples ECE elsewhere — and that study is undermined by conflicts of interest and apparent harness bugs (Mitra at 0.36 accuracy).

**4. Evaluation hygiene is fragile.** Three failure modes recur: (i) *contamination* — real-data pretraining/CPT corpora overlap standard benchmarks (Garg 2025 and TabDPT run serious audits; most others don't; Hoo 2024's Chronos comparison shows in-domain overlap silently inflates rivals); (ii) *meta-overfitting* — priors and checkpoints are selected on the same benchmark families they're evaluated on (Mitra's greedy prior selection on TabRepo; v2's checkpoint chosen on 27 real validation sets; PFNs4BO's own admission that its chosen acquisition wasn't best on test); (iii) *vendor science* — the two strongest models (v2.5, v3) are non-peer-reviewed corporate reports with undisclosed priors, unreproducible internal benchmarks, and non-commercial licenses that forbid the very production use (internal decision-making) the papers advertise. Tanna's Orion models topping Tanna's own leaderboard is the same problem in miniature.

**5. Inference compute is the un-amortized cost.** The "5,000× faster than tuned XGBoost" framing counts training, not deployment: per-prediction TabPFN is ~1000× slower than CatBoost (Hollmann 2025), ensembling and class/feature-decomposition tricks multiply passes, and full-context ICL means memory scales with the training set at *serving* time. Distillation (v2.5/v3) is the emerging answer but is itself proprietary.

**6. Interpretability and structural blind spots.** The Bayesian latent is never instantiated, so explanation is post-hoc: Rundel 2024's exact-retraining SHAP is elegant but demonstrated at 6 features/256 rows; Kolberg 2026's attention-as-importance is acknowledged as unvalidated. Deeper structural criticisms: the architecture cannot count repeated evidence (Müller 2025's coin-flip failure — a genuine violation of Bayesian updating), predictions cannot leave the convex hull of context targets (Hoo 2026 — no trend extrapolation), retrieval-like behavior is fragile under temporal shift and engineered features (Rubachev 2025 on TabReD/Homecredit — exactly credit-scoring conditions), and Klein & Hoffart 2026 argue the entire statistical framing misses rule-generated data (approval thresholds learned as fuzzy correlations). Helli 2024's honest result — drift-aware priors give large synthetic gains but marginal real-world ones — shows how hard the shift problem actually is.

**7. Regression is systematically second-class.** BETA, TabICL(v1), Drift-Resilient TabPFN, FairPFN, TabPFN-Wide, Real-TabPFN, TabTune, and nanoTabPFN are all classification-only. Given that regression (LGD-style bounded, bimodal targets) stresses the distributional heads hardest — and Do-PFN already documents bar-distribution underdispersion under forward-KL — this gap is not cosmetic.

## Open frontiers

**Resolving the prior question empirically.** The field needs the controlled experiment nobody has run: synthetic-prior improvement vs real-data CPT vs mixed, same base, same compute, evaluated in-domain and out-of-domain. Mitra's generalizability-matrix methodology (Zhang 2025) is the right tool — extended to ask whether a *domain* (e.g., credit risk) is "distinctive" relative to the synthetic prior, which would turn domain-specialization from a bet into a measurement. Qu 2026's finding of strong prior×architecture interaction (new architecture diverges on the old prior) warns that prior and model must be co-designed, so a v2.6 CPT recipe cannot be assumed to work on v3 without direct ablation—even when the engineering pipeline supports both.

**Regression and distributional outputs.** Extending the adaptation stack (CPT, BETA-style adapters, SFT recipes, drift priors) to regression is repeatedly flagged and never done (Kolberg 2026 names it explicitly). Head design is open too: Riemann/binned CE (v2) vs quantile-pinball (TabICLv2) vs GMM-NLL (Ma 2026 CausalFM) — no comparison exists on bounded/bimodal targets, and the convex-hull limitation plus bar-head underdispersion need fixes for any target that drifts.

**Calibration as a first-class benchmark axis.** Whether FT/CPT preserves the Bayesian calibration of the frozen model is the paradigm's most consequential open empirical question (Rubachev 2025's sharpening result vs Tanna 2025's TabPFN-stability result point in opposite directions). A standard ECE/Brier/coverage protocol across adaptation regimes — analogous to what TabArena did for accuracy — does not exist. Ditto fairness under adaptation (Tanna 2026's SFT-disparity swings; FairPFN's prior-level approach).

**Scaling rows AND features jointly.** v3's 1M×200 vs 100k×2k trade-off defines the frontier: no architecture handles both. Candidate directions from the corpus: learned context selection/data valuation (Rundel 2024) fused with retrieval-aligned training (TabDPT), linear/SSM attention (Müller 2025's roadmap), and hybrid compress-or-not routing by table shape.

**Non-i.i.d. structure.** Temporal drift (Helli 2024's 2nd-order SCMs; v3's temporal prior; TabReD failures), relational/multi-table data (TabPFN-REL; RelBench), and operational grounding (Klein & Hoffart 2026's rules-as-prior proposal) all break the exchangeable-rows assumption at the paradigm's core. For credit specifically, the through-the-door/reject-inference problem maps onto CausalFM's observational-context/interventional-target training template (Ma 2026) — an unexplored bridge between the causal-PFN line (Do-PFN, FairPFN, CausalFM) and domain deployment.

**Test-time compute and openness.** v3's "Thinking" mode shows test-time compute helps (+200 Elo) but is a closed black box — how to spend inference compute adaptively (Müller 2025 suggests RL-based allocation) is wide open. Meanwhile the ecosystem is bifurcating: closed frontier checkpoints with prohibitive licenses (v2.5/v3) vs open-but-behind alternatives (TabICLv2, TabDPT, nanoTabPFN as sandbox). Reproducible, domain-specialized CPT on open backbones — with calibration, contamination audits, and regression support — is precisely the niche the closed frontier leaves unoccupied, and where academic work (including CreditPFN's PD/LGD program) can still set the standard.

## Where CreditPFN sits

CreditPFN occupies the intersection of two currents in the TFM literature: real-data continued pretraining and domain specialization. Methodologically it is a direct application of the Real-TabPFN recipe (Garg 2025) — continued pretraining of TabPFN v2.x on curated real tables with L2-SP regularization toward the synthetic-prior initialization — but it narrows the corpus from Garg's 71 heterogeneous AMLB-style tables to a single vertical: credit-risk datasets covering PD (classification, TabPFN classifier) and LGD (regression, TabPFN regressor). The bet is that Garg's cross-domain gains compound when the pretraining distribution matches the deployment domain — the same bet Kolberg (2026) makes with TabPFN-Wide, though Kolberg specializes along an axis (high-dimensional omics via a synthetic feature-widening prior) rather than a domain, showing continued pretraining can reshape the prior itself. CreditPFN instead keeps the prior implicit and lets real credit data supply the domain structure, sidestepping the prior-engineering route that Mitra (Zhang 2025) takes, where synthetic prior mixtures are optimized for performance, diversity, and distinctiveness; Zhang's framework nonetheless explains what continued pretraining must add — density on data-generating processes (imbalanced binary targets, bounded bimodal LGD) that generic SCM priors underweight.

On adaptation mechanics, CreditPFN follows the evidence from Rubachev (2025) that full finetuning beats PEFT/LoRA for TabPFNv2 and works by sharpening context retrieval — while heeding the caveat that finetuned models grow unstable under temporal shift, precisely the regime of through-the-cycle credit portfolios. Finally, the Tanna (2025, 2026) benchmarks temper expectations: across TFMs, fine-tuning often degrades calibration even when accuracy holds, and TabPFN merely tolerates SFT best. For Basel-regulated PD, where calibrated probabilities feed capital requirements, this makes ECE/Brier tracking during continued pretraining a first-class evaluation criterion for CreditPFN, not an afterthought.

BeyondArena (Purucker 2026) sharpens the stakes from both sides. As warning: temporal splits — exactly the through-the-cycle deployment regime of PD/LGD models — are where TabPFN-2.6's in-context learning loses to tuned RealMLP and GBDTs, and credit data's high-cardinality categoricals and larger sample sizes are precisely the meta-features that predict a further TFM disadvantage (ρ=+0.47/+0.60). As mandate: the benchmark evaluates zero-training ICL only and explicitly lists TFM fine-tuning as untested, so continued pretraining on in-domain, temporally structured credit data targets the exact gap the paper exposes rather than contradicting it — and its calibration ablation (TabPFN-2.6 was one of only two models that post-hoc calibration made *worse*) corroborates that native TFM calibration is the differentiator continued pretraining must preserve, measured under temporal splits, not just discrimination.


## Appendix — one-card-per-paper reference

**Müller 2021 — Transformers Can Do Bayesian Inference (PFNs).** Foundational paper reducing Bayesian posterior-predictive approximation to supervised meta-training on synthetic datasets, executed at test time in a single forward pass. *Method:* Train a permutation-equivariant Transformer encoder on datasets sampled from a prior (GPs, BNNs, BNNs over architectures) to predict held-out labels via cross-entropy, which provably minimizes KL to the true PPD; regression uses a bucketed Riemann head. *Strength:* Theoretically grounded (loss = KL to true PPD, so more training cannot overfit), extremely fast (1,000–8,000× vs NUTS), and natively well-calibrated (ECE 0.025 vs 0.066 XGB). *Weakness:* Tiny scale — ~30 labeled rows, ≤60 features, balanced binary only, simplistic GP/BNN priors, no categoricals/missing values, and quadratic attention limits context.

**Hollmann 2023 — TabPFN v1.** First tabular foundation model: one pretrained Transformer classifies any small numerical table in a single tuning-free forward pass, matching 1h-budget AutoML. *Method:* PFN trained on 9.2M synthetic datasets from a mixed SCM+BNN prior with an Occam simplicity bias; 12-layer, 25.8M-param set-Transformer with train-train self-attention and test→train cross-attention, plus 32-way inference ensembling over feature/label rotations and power transforms. *Strength:* Instant (0.62s GPU, ~5,700× speedup), calibrated, and competitive with tuned GBDTs/AutoML on 18 OpenML-CC18 numerical datasets. *Weakness:* Hard caps (~1000 rows, 100 features, 10 classes, classification only) and degradation on categorical features, missing values, and uninformative features.

**Müller 2023 — PFNs4BO.** Shows PFNs are a flexible, amortized drop-in replacement for GP surrogates in Bayesian optimization, with no per-iteration hyperparameter fitting. *Method:* Trains PFNs on GP, HEBO+, and BNN priors; regression handled by the Riemann (bucketed) head with closed-form EI/PI/UCB on it, plus style-token user priors, spurious-dimension injection during prior-fitting, and input warping. *Strength:* Encodes priors GPs cannot (BNNs, user beliefs, irrelevant features) and beats all baselines on HPO-B while inference is a single forward pass. *Weakness:* Fragile when data has low likelihood under the prior, no joint sampling across points, validated only to ~1000 context points, and the chosen acquisition/scaling heuristics show honesty flags (validation-picked EI, benchmark-bug exploitation).

**van Breugel & van der Schaar 2024 — Why TFMs Should Be a Research Priority.** ICML position paper coining "Large Tabular Models" and arguing tabular foundation models deserve LLM-scale attention while remaining feasible on academic compute. *Method:* No model or experiments; specifies four desiderata (mixed types, cross-dataset modelling, textual context, column-order invariance), favors attention-based conditional generative models, and surveys data (TabLib: 627M tables) and prior cross-table work. *Strength:* Crisp reusable design/evaluation checklist (D1–D4, adaptation and benchmark taxonomies) that effectively framed the TFM research agenda. *Weakness:* Purely argumentative — no numbers — and confined to single independent tables, with diversity of web corpora and coverage of sensitive/proprietary domains left unproven.

**Helli 2024 — Drift-Resilient TabPFN.** Adapts TabPFN to temporal distribution shift purely by changing the synthetic prior, so the model extrapolates decision boundaries into unseen future domains. *Method:* A secondary "2nd-order SCM" maps the temporal domain index to sparse edge-weight shifts in the base SCM prior (sparse-mechanism-shift hypothesis), with Time2Vec domain encoding; training objective and inference are unchanged, pretrained on 30.7M synthetic datasets. *Strength:* Unifies covariate/prior/concept shift with strong OOD calibration gains (ECE 0.119→0.091) and no deploy-time tuning at ~11s inference. *Weakness:* Real-world gains are small and often within confidence intervals, ground-truth domain indices are required, and it inherits v1's O(n²) small-data ceiling with classification only.

**Hoo 2024 — TabPFN Outperforms Specialized Time-Series Forecasters.** Reframes univariate forecasting as tabular regression on timestamp features and beats time-series foundation models with zero time-series pretraining. *Method:* Feed history rows as ICL context to the TabPFN v2 regression model (~11M params) with only a running index plus sine/cosine calendar features; take the median of the predictive distribution for MASE. *Strength:* Wins on the 17 true zero-shot datasets against Chronos-Large (0.772 vs 0.841 relative MASE) with a tiny model and no contamination risk, and ablations show the gain is TabPFN-specific (CatBoost swap fails). *Weakness:* Severe per-series latency (avg 2,210s vs Chronos-Mini 8s), point-forecast evaluation only, local univariate modelling with no covariates, and reliance on a closed hosted checkpoint.

**Rundel 2024 — Interpretable ML for TabPFN.** Re-derives standard IML methods to exploit TabPFN's in-context learning, where "retraining" on any data/feature subset is just one forward pass. *Method:* Batched ICE/PD/ALE, tractable LOCO, exact-retraining Kernel SHAP (column-restricted contexts, avoiding the feature-independence assumption), Data-Shapley context selection to beat the 1024-row cap, and gradient-based sensitivity analysis, released as `tabpfn_iml`. *Strength:* Exact subset-retraining yields lower Shapley approximation error and variance than imputation-based SHAP, making faithful attributions cheap. *Weakness:* Binary classification only, tiny experiments (≤256 rows, 6 features), TabPFN v1 base, and the Data-Shapley context optimization costs ~10,000 forward passes for marginal gains.

**Garg 2025 — Real-TabPFN (continued pretraining on real data).** Shows a short continued-pretraining stage on curated real tables improves TabPFNv2's ICL beyond its synthetic-only prior. *Method:* CPT of the released v2 checkpoint on 71 large (10k–100k row) OpenML/Kaggle datasets with cross-entropy + L2-SP (λ=0.003) against base weights, LR 3e-7, 20k steps, batch 1, 60/40 context/query splits up to 20k rows, plus a 5-tier contamination protocol. *Strength:* Cheap and reproducible (single consumer GPU) with statistically significant gains (normalized ROC-AUC 0.954→0.976, p=0.0045), better calibration, and clean ablations isolating dataset size as the driver (small CommonCrawl tables hurt). *Weakness:* Workshop-length, classification only (no regression), 29 small eval datasets, modest absolute ROC gains, and no sensitivity analysis on λ/LR/steps or parameter-efficient variants.

**Hollmann 2025 — TabPFN v2 (Nature).** The scaled tabular foundation model: one transformer, pretrained on ~100M+ synthetic SCM datasets, beats tuned GBDT ensembles on small tables in seconds with no per-dataset training. *Method:* Cell-as-token architecture with alternating feature- and sample-attention (12 layers), synthetic prior from scale-free DAGs with NN/tree/discretization mechanisms and injected missingness, cross-entropy PPD objective, Riemann regression head over ~5,000 bins, and 4–8-way pre/post-processing ensembles with T=0.9 calibration. *Strength:* SOTA small-data accuracy for both classification and regression (normalized ROC 0.952 vs 0.822 tuned CatBoost; 3,000–5,140× speedups) with native distributional outputs, robustness to messy data, and foundation-model abilities (fine-tuning, generation, embeddings). *Weakness:* Hard caps (≤10k rows, ≤500 features, ≤10 classes), memory linear in table size, slower per-sample inference than trees, weak very-non-smooth regression, and unreleased prior-generation code.

**Liu & Ye 2025 — TabPFN Unleashed (BETA).** Lifts TabPFN v1's feature/class caps and cuts both bias and variance via a tiny learnable input encoder plus bagging, keeping the frozen backbone and single-pass efficiency. *Method:* Train only a 2-layer MLP encoder (with periodic numerical embeddings) mapping raw features into a fixed 100-dim latent for frozen TabPFN; K Batch-Ensemble encoder paths plus bootstrapped ICL contexts reduce variance, and ECOC handles >10 classes. *Strength:* Genuinely lightweight (0.6MB trainable, 0.91s inference) and best average rank on 186 TALENT datasets against XGBoost/CatBoost/LocalPFN/MixturePFN with default hyperparameters. *Weakness:* Classification only on TabPFN v1, assumes IID (no drift handling), keeps a fixed 1000-row context, and the encoder can overfit on very small datasets while discarding raw-feature alignment with the prior.


**Müller 2025 — The Future of Bayesian Prediction Is Prior-Fitted.** Position paper arguing PFNs — transformers trained on synthetic prior samples to approximate posterior predictive distributions via ICL — will supersede MCMC/VI/GPs for prediction, converting abundant compute into Bayesian quality in data-scarce regimes. *Method:* Cross-entropy training on held-out points of prior-sampled datasets minimizes expected KL to the true PPD; priors are declarative sampling algorithms (BNN, GP, SCM/TabPFN), served by a permutation-invariant encoder transformer with single-forward-pass inference. *Strength:* Explicitly endorses post-pretraining fine-tuning on related-domain datasets and names calibrated uncertainty a core PFN advantage — direct strategic justification for CreditPFN. *Weakness:* Conceptual/roadmap only, with honestly stated open problems: ill-defined support, O(n²) attention, inability to count duplicated evidence, and degraded handling of heavy-tailed/heterogeneous features.

**Pfefferle 2025 — nanoTabPFN.** A <500-line educational reimplementation of the TabPFN v2 architecture and training loop ("nanoGPT of TFMs") that trains in ~60s on one consumer GPU. *Method:* Reuses TabICL's synthetic prior (dumped to HDF5 for fast iteration; 150 rows × 5 features × 2 classes per dataset), faithful 2D feature×datapoint bi-attention with train/test masking, schedule-free AdamW with near-zero weight decay (best HPO wd = 1e-7). *Strength:* Exposes in readable form the exact mechanisms continued pretraining touches (encoders, attention masking, optimizer recipe) and corroborates the wd≈0 choice. *Weakness:* Classification-only, no categorical/missing-value handling, tiny-data regime, and the "160,000× faster / beats TabPFN" claims are apples-to-oranges (model trained to match the eval scale).

**Qu 2025 — TabICL.** Scales tabular ICL to 500K rows on affordable hardware by collapsing the column dimension once, matching TabPFNv2 accuracy (rank 6.95 vs 7.17 on TALENT) at 1.5–10× speed. *Method:* Enriched SCM prior (70% MLP / 30% XGBoost-tree SCMs, richer activations); three-transformer pipeline — Set-Transformer distribution-aware column embedding, RoPE row-wise transformer with CLS tokens, then a 12-layer ICL transformer — trained with a 3-stage size curriculum (final stage freezes all but the ICL module). *Strength:* Order-of-magnitude row scalability with strong calibration (LogLoss), plus a portable "freeze backbone, train ICL head" adaptation blueprint and memory-offload inference tricks. *Weakness:* Classification-only (no LGD path), RoPE breaks column-permutation invariance (patched by ensembling), and its architecture diverges from TabPFNv2 so recipes are not directly transferable.

**Robertson 2025 — Do-PFN.** Extends the PFN recipe from prediction to causal effect estimation, amortizing conditional interventional distributions p(y|do(t),x) from observational data alone — no graph, interventional data, or unconfoundedness needed. *Method:* Pretrains a tiny (7.3M-param) TabPFN-style transformer on millions of sampled SCMs, each paired with observational context and interventional queries generated on the mutilated graph with shared noise; NLL training provably minimizes forward-KL to the true CID. *Strength:* Beats meta-learners/causal forests especially when unconfoundedness is violated, with principled uncertainty that correctly inflates in unidentifiable cases. *Weakness:* Binary treatments only, additive-noise prior biases toward small effects, degrades on graphs >10 nodes, and the bar-distribution/forward-KL head is slightly underdispersed — a caveat for LGD-style regression calibration.

**Robertson 2025 — FairPFN.** Shows a PFN can learn counterfactually fair prediction purely from synthetic causal pretraining, with no user-supplied causal graph. *Method:* TabPFNv1-style sparse-MLP SCM prior with a binary exogenous protected attribute; each SCM yields a biased dataset and (via zeroing A's outgoing weights) a fair target, so the transformer learns the map X_biased → Y_fair in-context. *Strength:* Demonstrates a prior can encode a task transformation (not just a data distribution), with honest failure-mode ablations that gracefully revert to a plain classifier — a useful evaluation pattern. *Weakness:* Single binary exogenous attribute, binary classification only, two real datasets with circular DoWhy-based "ground truth," and no calibration analysis.

**Rubachev 2025 — On Finetuning Tabular Foundation Models.** Controlled comparison of TabPFNv2 adaptation strategies finding full fine-tuning matches all PEFT variants in accuracy while converging fastest, reaching SoTA on academic i.i.d. benchmarks (FT×5 ensemble, mean rank 1.05). *Method:* Full FT vs LoRA/last-layers/LN+embeddings/feature-embeddings on datasets up to ~50K rows with tuned LR grids; mechanistic probes (attention-weighted kNN, attention entropy) show FT sharpens the model's implicit retrieval. *Strength:* Rare mechanistic explanation plus practical knobs to copy (LR logspace grid, 1024-object prediction batch, patience-16 early stopping, FT×5 ensembling), with honest negative results under temporal shift. *Weakness:* Accuracy-only evaluation — no AUC, logloss, or calibration — leaving open whether retrieval sharpening harms PD calibration, and instability on shifted TabReD data (incl. Homecredit) warns against i.i.d.-only evaluation for credit.

**Tanna 2025 — TabTune.** Unified scikit-learn-style library for inference and adaptation of 7 TFMs, plus an empirical study showing adaptation success is strongly architecture-dependent. *Method:* Common pipeline (model-aware preprocessing, four regimes: zero-shot ICL, episodic meta-learning, full SFT, LoRA-PEFT) benchmarked on TALENT/OpenML-CC18/TabZilla with calibration and fairness metrics as first-class outputs. *Strength:* External validation that TabPFN uniquely tolerates full SFT (rank 1.97) while keeping ECE stable (0.027–0.047) — whereas SFT collapses TabICL — directly supporting a full-fine-tuning recipe with calibration claims for PD. *Weakness:* Classification-only, single untuned defaults per strategy, author-model conflict of interest (Orion), no significance tests, and per-dataset fine-tuning only — nothing on corpus-level continued pretraining or LGD.

**Ye 2025 — A Closer Look at TabPFN v2.** Independent mechanistic analysis and retraining-free extension of the frozen TabPFNv2 checkpoint across 300 datasets, explaining how random feature tokens enable heterogeneity handling. *Method:* Probing (PCA/attention analysis) shows randomized tokens act as one-hot feature IDs so the model infers attribute relations in-context; divide-and-conquer variants (feature bagging, decimal class encoding, embedding+LogReg, decision-tree routing) extend it to high-dim/many-class/large-N regimes. *Strength:* Rigorous honest evaluation providing strong frozen-model credit baselines (e.g., default_of_credit_card 82.6, credit-g 79.5) and a leakage-safe leave-one-fold-out embedding diagnostic; documents the large-N degradation that motivates continued pretraining. *Weakness:* No training-side contribution (causes hypothesized, not tested), extensions multiply inference cost yet still lose to CatBoost/RealMLP at scale, and evaluation is accuracy/RMSE only with no calibration metrics.

**Zhang 2025 — Mitra.** First quantitative framework for designing synthetic prior mixtures, replacing heuristic SCM+tree mixing with measured criteria; the resulting 72M-param model tops Elo on 137 classification datasets and TabRepo regression. *Method:* Pretrains one TFM per candidate prior and scores each via a Generalizability Matrix (performance, diversity, distinctiveness), yielding a 50% SCM / 50% tree-prior mix (incl. a novel directly-sampled RF) on a TabPFNv2-style 2D-attention backbone; contributes 8-fold CV bagging of finetuned TFMs. *Strength:* Its explanation of why fine-tuning helps — compensating the pretrain/downstream distribution gap — is exactly the CreditPFN thesis at domain level, and the bagged-CV ensemble, regression checkpoint, and generalizability-matrix methodology are directly portable. *Weakness:* Thin margins over Attic (8 Elo, overlapping CIs), tiny pretraining regime (16 features/640 rows) that loses to TabPFNv2 on wide regression, greedy prior selection risking meta-overfitting, and no calibration analysis.

**Zhang 2025 — TabPFN: One Model to Rule Them All.** Statistical reframing of TabPFNv2 as amortized approximate Bayesian inference, plus independent evidence that the untuned model matches or beats bespoke SOTA on semi-supervised M-estimation, CATE estimation, and covariate shift. *Method:* No new model — plugs TabPFNv2 as-is into imputation-debiasing, S/T/X/DR meta-learners, and unadjusted shift prediction; crisply derives the PFN loss as an unbiased KL-to-PPD estimator over the SCM prior. *Strength:* Unique calibration/robustness evidence — near-nominal coverage, label-noise consistency to ρ=0.4, and covariate-shift wins — bolstering PD calibration and through-the-cycle drift claims, and framing v2's Bayesian behavior as worth preserving against catastrophic forgetting. *Weakness:* Purely empirical with no theory for the implicit-regularization benefits, and documents the exact ceilings CreditPFN must work around: MSE plateau at n≳10K, ~500-feature cap, dense high-d regression failures, and no weighted-loss support.


**Grinsztajn 2026 — TabPFN-2.5.** Scales the synthetic-prior TabPFN paradigm to 50k rows × 2k features (~20× more cells) and claims SOTA on TabArena, with a Real-TabPFN-2.5 variant continued-pretrained on 43 real datasets. *Method:* Same alternating dual-attention architecture as v2 but deeper (18/24 layers), feature groups of 3, 64 learned "thinking rows," and ~50 hyperparameters tuned via a TabPFNv2 regression surrogate ("TabPFN-tunes-TabPFN"); adds threshold tuning, temperature scaling, and a proprietary distillation engine to MLP/tree students for low-latency deployment. *Strength:* Verified leap over v2 (Wilcoxon p<0.0001, "never much worse") on public TabArena, with Real-TabPFN-2.5 defaults beating every tuned+ensembled competitor on classification. *Weakness:* Corporate, non-peer-reviewed report whose two key ingredients (new synthetic prior, distillation engine) are undisclosed, with non-commercial-licensed weights and no calibration (ECE/Brier) metrics despite calibration claims.

**Grinsztajn 2026 — TabPFN-3 Technical Report.** Purely synthetic-pretrained TFM that scales to 1M rows on a single H100 and tops TabArena/TALENT, abandoning v2.x's dual attention for a TabICLv2-style row-compression design. *Method:* Three-stage architecture (inducing-point feature-distribution embedding → 4 CLS tokens per row → 24-layer ICL transformer with multi-query cross-attention and QASSMax), an attention-based retrieval decoder for classification and a 5000-bin bar-distribution head for regression, trained on >8T synthetic tokens from an SCM prior extended with temporal, OOD, spatial, and many-class components. *Strength:* Genuine scaling breakthrough with exact-equivalence row chunking, calibrated full predictive distributions, up to 20× faster inference, and zero real-data contamination. *Weakness:* Vendor report with no peer review or prior ablations, a closed-box "Thinking" mode, a cell-budget bottleneck when rows and features are both large (where Real-TabPFN-2.5 can still win), and a license barring commercial/production use.

**Hoo 2026 — From Tables to Time.** Shows a frozen TabPFN-v2 regressor does competitive zero-shot probabilistic forecasting when time series are recast as tabular regression with engineered temporal features. *Method:* Each timestep becomes a row; features are a running index, 17 calendar sin/cos features, top-5 FFT seasonal components, and optional known-future covariates; the full horizon is predicted in one non-autoregressive forward pass with no finetuning. *Strength:* Cleanest evidence that the synthetic prior transfers across modalities — best overall on the fev-bench covariate subset and on par with 100×-larger TSFMs on GIFT-Eval, with calibrated quantiles for free. *Weakness:* Structurally cannot extrapolate trends (predictions stay in the convex hull of observed targets), is ~30× slower than Chronos-Bolt, and the covariate comparison is somewhat favorable-by-construction.

**Klein & Hoffart 2026 — Position: TFMs Need Grounding in Systemic Contexts.** Argues TFMs treat tables as isolated statistical objects and miss the operational context (business rules, validation code, workflows) that generated them — e.g., learning a fuzzy ~$4,800 threshold where the real rule is `amount >= 5000`. *Method:* Proposes (without implementation) FMSLTs with dual-phase training — pretrain on code–data pairs and synthetic rule→row tasks, then zero-shot inference with retrieved operational artifacts in context — plus an "Operational Turing Test" benchmark requiring artifact citation. *Strength:* Sharp, well-referenced framing of a real gap, generalizing the PFN synthetic-prior philosophy to operational logic with an honest alternative-views section. *Weakness:* Zero empirical evidence — no architecture, loss, benchmark, or numbers — and the multi-table+code synthetic generation and provenance mapping it requires are acknowledged open problems.

**Kolberg 2026 — TabPFN-Wide.** Extends TabPFNv2 to extreme feature counts (tested to 60k) via continued pretraining on synthetically widened data, enabling HDLSS biomedical tasks without feature reduction. *Method:* Full-parameter continued pretraining (lr 1e-5, wd 1e-4, cosine+warmup, 10k steps) of the v2 classifier on TabICL-prior base tasks inflated by a feature-widening prior — sparse random linear maps plus noise for continuous features, donor-column copies for categoricals — with one model per max width; preprocessing disabled so feature→label attention serves as interpretability. *Strength:* Large AUROC gains on multi-omics (e.g., BRCA 0.733→0.971) and a 92% HDLSS win rate with no catastrophic forgetting (Spearman ρ=0.9935 on original-range tasks). *Weakness:* Classifier-only (regressor explicitly left open), the recipe failed to train TabICL, attention-as-importance is unvalidated, and no calibration analysis is reported.

**Ma 2026 — Foundation Models for Causal Inference via PFNs.** A recipe (CausalFM) for training one PFN per identifiable causal setting (back-door, front-door, IV), with a theorem showing mixed non-identifiable priors yield asymptotically non-informative posteriors. *Method:* Constrained "C-SCM" priors fix graph structure and identifiability assumptions while TabPFN-style BNN assignments generate data; a modified PFN NLL trains the model to predict interventional CATE targets from observational context, decoded through a 5-component Gaussian-mixture head. *Strength:* Clean identifiability-vs-estimation separation backed by an impossibility theorem, first PFN handling front-door and IV, fully disclosed prior hyperparameters, and ~0.5 s inference. *Weakness:* Evaluated only on synthetic/semi-synthetic data resembling its own prior (losing clearly to CausalPFN on ACIC2016), requires one model per setting, and claims Bayesian uncertainty without ever evaluating calibration or coverage.

**Ma 2026 — TabDPT.** Demonstrates that from-scratch pretraining on real tables with self-supervision can match TabPFN v2 — the strongest evidence that real data carries signal synthetic priors miss — and that TFMs obey LLM-style scaling laws. *Method:* A 78M-parameter TabPFNv1-style row-as-token transformer trained on 123 contamination-audited OpenML datasets (32M rows) via column-masking SSL (random columns become pseudo-targets) with FAISS retrieval-aligned training so contexts are local neighborhoods matching inference. *Strength:* Fully open (weights, code, data list) with an explicit contamination audit, an honest "Bitter Lessons" failure appendix, and the first clean Chinchilla-style TFM scaling analysis (α=0.42, β=0.39). *Weakness:* Capped at 100 features/10 classes (requiring lossy PCA and base-C tricks), margins over TabPFN v2 are small and mostly CI-overlapping, no calibration metrics are reported, and per-query retrieval complicates deployment.

**Qu 2026 — TabICLv2.** Open, cheaply pretrained (≈24.5 H100 GPU-days) synthetic-only TFM that beats RealTabPFN-2.5 tuned+ensembled on TabArena while running 10.6× faster and natively scaling to 1M rows. *Method:* Three-stage set-transformer→row-embedding→ICL architecture with repeated feature grouping, target-aware embeddings, QASSMax for long-context generalization, and a 999-quantile pinball-loss regression head; a redesigned SCM prior (random Cauchy graphs, 8 function types, ExtraTrees-based data filtering) trained in three stages with the Muon optimizer. *Strength:* Untuned SOTA with fully open weights and zero real-data leakage concerns, and ablations showing the prior is the largest effect (with strong architecture×prior interaction). *Weakness:* Mean-imputes missing values (harmful for informative missingness), ignores column-name semantics, never reports calibration despite log-loss metrics, and the prior/pretraining code was not yet released.

**Tanna 2026 — Exploring Fine-Tuning for Tabular Foundation Models.** First large-scale empirical study of when per-dataset fine-tuning helps TFMs, finding zero-shot ICL already competitive and full SFT frequently harmful. *Method:* Six TFMs (TabPFN v2, TabICL, Orion-MSP/BiX, TabDPT, Mitra) evaluated across four regimes (zero-shot, episodic meta-learning, full SFT, LoRA r=8/α=16) on 245 datasets plus 9 fairness datasets, jointly measuring accuracy, calibration (ECE/MCE/Brier), and fairness. *Strength:* Rare joint accuracy+calibration+fairness lens showing TabPFN is uniquely tolerant of full fine-tuning while preserving calibration (ECE 0.0287 vs 0.0276 zero-shot), with concrete regime-dependent guidelines. *Weakness:* A 4-page paper with barely tuned recipes (single LoRA config, ≤10 epochs), an apparent Mitra integration bug and duplicated PEFT tables undermining harness trust, an author conflict when their own Orion-MSP tops the leaderboard, and no comparison to corpus-level continued pretraining.



**Purucker et al. 2026 — Beyond IID: How General Are Tabular Foundation Models, Really?** BeyondArena (142 datasets) shows TFM in-context learning dominates tiny/small IID data but loses to tuned MLPs/GBDTs on temporal, grouped, large, high-dimensional, high-cardinality tasks. *Method:* Evaluates in-context learning of 3 TFMs (TabPFN-2.6, TabICLv2, TabDPT) against 8 default/tuned+ensembled baselines (CatBoost, XGBoost, LightGBM, RealMLP, TabM, RF, ET, Linear) on BeyondArena — 142 datasets hand-curated from 1,128 spanning IID/temporal/grouped splits, 100–1M rows, text and high-cardinality features (~$50k compute); also releases the DataFoundry curation framework, integrated into TabArena. *Strength:* Exceptional protocol rigor — application-appropriate outer/inner splits (ablations show IID splits distort grouped rankings to Kendall τ=0.49–0.60), Friedman/Wilcoxon significance testing, per-regime practitioner recommendations. *Weakness:* Tests ICL only (fine-tuning explicitly left as future work), non-IID sub-benchmarks are small (18 grouped / 21 temporal datasets, grouped rankings noisy), and TabPFN-2.6/TabDPT results beyond 100k rows are Random-Forest-imputed — while the authors include TabPFN-2.6's own developers (Prior Labs).
